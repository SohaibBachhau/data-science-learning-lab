# Mean Reversion

Mean reversion means that when a stationary process moves unusually far above or below its normal level, it tends to move back toward that normal level over time.

This does not mean the series moves straight back to the mean in the next period. New shocks can push it around, and the return can be gradual.

The important idea is that the effect of being far from the mean does not grow forever. In a stable AR model, part of the deviation carries forward, but less than the full amount, so the deviation gradually fades unless new shocks keep pushing the process away again.

A useful example is a series with a long-run mean of 5. If it jumps to 9, tomorrow may still be expected to be above 5, but usually not as far above 5 as today. Over several periods, the expected path moves back toward 5.

A useful way to say it is:

> Mean reversion is the tendency of a stable time series to return toward its usual long-run level after a temporary deviation.

## One-sentence answer

Mean reversion means that unusually high or low values tend to move back toward the process's long-run average over time.
