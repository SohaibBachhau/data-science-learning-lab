# Conditional versus Unconditional

An unconditional quantity describes the process without conditioning on the specific information we currently have.

For example, the unconditional mean tells us the long-run center of the process. It answers a question like:

> Where does this series tend to be on average overall?

A conditional quantity uses information that is already known.

The conditional mean answers a question like:

> Given what happened yesterday, what should I expect today?

These two means can be very different without contradicting each other.

Suppose a stationary series normally revolves around 5, but yesterday it happened to be 8. If the process is persistent, today's best prediction may still be well above 5. The unconditional mean remains 5, while the conditional mean reflects the unusually high recent observation.

The same idea applies to variance. Unconditional variance describes overall variability in the process. Conditional variance describes how uncertain the next value is given the information already available.

A useful way to remember the difference is:

> Unconditional = the general behavior of the process.

> Conditional = the behavior we expect given what we know right now.

## One-sentence answer

The unconditional mean is the long-run average of the process, while the conditional mean is the best expected value given current information.
